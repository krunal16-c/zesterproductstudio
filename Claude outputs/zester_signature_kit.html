Signature Kit

Four signatures for Krunal Chavda, built on the Zester design system: one accent (#FFD400), zero radius, no images. Which one you use depends on who is receiving it.

Why this is short

A signature is a credential, and credentials work by restraint. Logos, social icons, quotes and legal disclaimers read as junior corporate, and every image in a signature is one more thing to get blocked, stretched or turned into a phantom attachment. Founders and operating executives sign off in three or four lines. So does this.

Accent#FFD400 as a 3px machined rule. Never as body text on a light ground, which is your own system's rule; links drop to #7a6000 so they pass contrast on cream and white.
TypeIBM Plex Sans for the name, IBM Plex Mono for contact data. Both degrade to Helvetica and Courier where the fonts are absent, which in email is most of the time.
GeometryRadius 0 throughout, matching the shape lock on the site.
ImagesNone. No logo, no headshot, no tracking pixel. Pure HTML and text.

Installing in Gmail

  1. Replace +91 98765 43210 with your real number in whichever block you are using. It appears twice in the HTML: once in the tel: link and once as visible text.
  2. Confirm partnerships@zesterproductstudio.com is the mailbox you actually created. If you went with a different local part, change it in the mailto: and the visible text.
  3. Copy the HTML, then paste into Gmail → Settings → See all settings → General → Signature. Paste renders it; Gmail keeps the inline styles.
  4. Set Signature defaults to use the standard block on new mail and no signature on replies, then paste the reply or single-line version manually when a thread runs long.
  5. Send one test to a non-Google address and check the yellow rule survives. Outlook is the one that most often eats borders; if it does, the signature still reads correctly without it.

What not to add

  • A logo image. Blocked by default in most clients, arrives as a phantom attachment in Outlook, and adds a spam signal on a young domain.
  • Social icons. Nobody has ever clicked one in a signature. They read as marketing.
  • A confidentiality disclaimer. Legally close to worthless on outbound mail and a reliable tell of a junior sender.
  • "Sent from my iPhone" left on. Turn it off in the iOS mail settings.
  • A quote or a tagline. Your positioning belongs in the email body, where it is being read.
  • A booking link, at least until the campaign is producing replies. In a first cold email it is the single most common reason for a spam flag.
