# The Missing Middle
## Why 10,500 Indian factories cannot buy the thing they need

**Zester Product Studio · Market research · 5 September 2026**

---

## What this is

The evidence behind Zester's positioning, with sources, so anyone reading the pitch can check the reasoning rather than take it on trust.

Two conventions run through it. Every claim carries its source. Every claim we could not verify is marked as such, including the ones that would have helped us. Section 7 records a thesis we built, tested and killed, because a research document that only contains findings that survived is a sales brochure.

---

## 1. The market is a barbell and the middle is empty

Indian industrial AI has exactly two purchase shapes.

**One end: product companies selling licences.**

| Company | FY25 revenue | Raised | What that implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detect Technologies | Rs 46.4 Cr | $43.3M, nothing since 2022 | Slow enterprise sales cycle |
| Ripik.AI | Rs 23.5 Cr, +92% | Contested figures | Growing fast off a small base |
| Infinite Uptime | Not disclosed | ~$65M, ~800 plants | Sensor-led, hardware moat |
| SwitchOn | Not disclosed | ~$13.3M | 170 lines, sub-150 micron inspection |

**The other end: services firms.** L&T Technology Services closed FY26 at $1.23bn with total contract value above $850M and multiple deals over $10M. Tata Elxsi posted Rs 993.8 Cr in a quarter. Accenture acquired Flutura in 2023 and is acquiring Industries eXcellence Group, consolidating the Siemens implementation channel.

**Between them, nothing.** A specific search for embedded, fractional or forward-deployed engineering models in Indian manufacturing returned US software agencies and no domestic equivalent.

**Why the middle stays empty is structural.** A licence company must build only what repeats across a hundred plants, so a Morbi ceramics line with an idiosyncratic problem is unsellable to it at any price. A firm carrying 23,000 people cannot profitably staff a six-week engagement. Neither is being lazy. Both are behaving correctly given their model.

The revenue figures are the sharpest evidence. Rs 23.5 Cr and Rs 46.4 Cr are the category leaders. **The licence model is not reaching this market**, and the funding pattern (Detect has raised nothing since 2022) suggests investors noticed before the market did.

**Local integrators do not fill the gap.** Ahmedabad has a dense automation sector: Micon, Vbtech, Digicon, Maven, Zenex, Themis, Vis-Con and others. They are panel builders and PLC/SCADA houses with real depth in wiring and controls, and no capability in machine learning, data engineering or MLOps. The firms advertising "AI" appear to mean off-the-shelf vision cameras. No Gujarat firm was found positioning on embedded engineers plus edge AI plus custom build.

## 2. The buyer has already told us what is wrong

PwC surveyed Indian MSMEs and published *Unlocking the AI Edge for MSMEs* in March 2026. The recorded complaints, in order:

> "The consultant did a great pilot but did not have any idea about how to scale it."

> "Show me the real value AI will deliver and the time frame."

Alongside these: explicit fear of **vendor lock-in**, **hidden integration costs** and **high subscription fees**. **91%** say AI must be affordable to adopt. **87%** expect productivity gains, so appetite is not the constraint.

There is also an awareness chasm worth sitting with. Across the annual reports of India's 25 largest listed MSME manufacturers for FY23 to FY25, "AI" appears **29 times**. For the 25 largest large-caps, **963 times**.

**Why this matters commercially.** Three objections, and an engagement structure answers all three by construction. A fixed end date answers the abandoned pilot. Client ownership of the code answers lock-in and subscription fees. No licence business can adopt the ownership clause without destroying its own revenue model, which makes it a structural position rather than a feature.

## 3. Pilot purgatory, quantified

About **70%** of manufacturers have AI initiatives running. Roughly **one third** get past pilot into production. Around **40%** report enterprise-level EBIT impact despite ~80% using AI somewhere.

McKinsey attributes the gap to the **last IT/OT mile**: inconsistent data across sites, legacy ERP and control systems without APIs, and workflows never redesigned around the model.

**A caution on numbers you will see quoted elsewhere.** The widely-circulated "80% of AI projects fail in Indian manufacturing" traces to a vendor blog with no methodology. The "88 to 95% of pilots never reach production" figure could only be traced to secondary aggregators, not a primary study. **Neither will survive scrutiny from a CFO and neither is used in our materials.**

**Indian adoption baseline**, from the India SME Forum META Report Card 2025, covering 7,835 MSMEs across 28 states:

- **53.8%** use at least one digital tool
- **46.2%** are fully offline
- **28%** consider digital mission-critical
- **97.3%** are unaware of government digitalisation schemes

That last number is a distribution opportunity rather than a market-size problem. A vendor who arrives explaining which scheme pays for the work is doing something almost nobody else does.

## 4. Why now: three dated triggers

Sentiment does not move factories. Deadlines with penalties do.

**CBAM definitive phase, live 1 January 2026.** Certificates must be purchased and surrendered, first declaration 30 September 2027, penalty EUR 100 per tonne. GTRI estimates Indian steel and aluminium exporters face 15 to 22% price pressure. **The operative detail: a plant without verified plant-level emissions data is assigned EU default values running 30 to 80% above actual.** From 2026 only ISO-14065 accredited auditors can verify. That is a data collection problem with a price attached and a fixed date.

**New labour codes, in force 21 November 2025.** Overtime pays **double**. New muster roll, wage register and overtime record formats. Unified e-registration within 60 days. Every hour of unplanned overtime now costs twice what it did, which doubles the return on any scheduling or labour-tracking system without changing the system.

**Time-of-day electricity tariffs.** Maharashtra runs a 20 to 30% discount during solar hours and a **25% surcharge at evening peak**. Load shifting has arithmetic payback. Gujarat held tariffs flat for FY26, so this argument is Maharashtra-first.

**One trigger to treat carefully.** Private capex rose 67% to Rs 7.7 lakh crore in H1 FY26, which reads like a boom. Adani and Reliance are **28% of it**, capacity utilisation sits at 75.6%, and mid-market capex is described as "noticeably patchier." The revival is real and it is not reaching this segment. We do not build messaging on it.

## 5. The state pays most of the invoice

The affordability objection has a public answer, and 97.3% of MSMEs do not know it exists.

| Scheme | Pays | Cap | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gujarat Technology Acquisition** | 65% of cost | Rs 50 lakh | Framed as technology acquisition |
| **Maharashtra MIISP 2025** | 50% of capital equipment | Rs 25 lakh | Policy runs Dec 2025 to Dec 2030 |
| **MSME LEAN** (Intermediate / Advanced) | 90% of consultant fees | Rs 1.2L / Rs 2.4L | Requires QCI or NPC empanelment and a group of 4 to 10 units |
| **Gujarat Energy Conservation** | 75% of audit, 25% of equipment | Rs 20 lakh | Pairs with the tariff argument |
| **MSME ZED** | Handholding consultancy | Rs 2 lakh | Pairs with LEAN |

**Two negatives that matter more than the positives.**

**SAMARTH Udyog Bharat 4.0 gives no money to anyone.** PIB, 13 December 2024, states it plainly: no financial assistance is given to any industry including MSMEs for adopting Industry 4.0 technologies. Its five centres offer awareness, training and demonstrations. Valuable as a credibility channel, useless as funding.

**RPTUAS, the Schedule M upgrade subsidy, does not cover software.** Eligible spend is production equipment, HVAC, water systems, cleanrooms, testing labs, stability chambers, effluent treatment and certification. Uptake is poor: 100+ applications, 7 MSME approvals.

**Price anchoring.** Indian mid-market ERP implementations run **Rs 20 to 60 lakh**. That is the number already in a promoter's head for "a system." A six-week sprint at Rs 8 to 15 lakh reads as reasonable against it. Quoted as a day rate, the same work invites comparison with a local integrator at a third of the price.

## 6. Distribution is geographic, not digital

The World Economic Forum's assessment of Indian MSME AI adoption identifies **cluster-led deployment and peer learning** as the single biggest adoption motivator. India, it argues, "lacks translation at scale" rather than technology.

This favours a locally embedded studio over a Bengaluru SaaS vendor, and it makes geography a compounding asset. Three plants in one Ankleshwar estate makes the fourth close to free.

**The channels that follow from it**, in order of return per rupee:

1. **Vendor Development Programmes.** The Vibrant Gujarat VDP put Zydus, Lupin, GSFC, ONGC, Maruti Suzuki, Morris Garages, ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel and Elecon procurement in one room for an afternoon. Near-zero cost.
2. **Estate association seminars.** Ankleshwar, Vapi, Vatva, Dahej, Panoli. Plant heads attend because it is free and local.
3. **Founder-led LinkedIn.** Manufacturing runs 19 to 24% P90 engagement, above Technology. India is LinkedIn's second-largest market.
4. **Association membership as access.** Gujarat Chemical Association Rs 10,000 lifetime, ~700 members. CII ~Rs 13,000 in year one.
5. **MSME LEAN empanelment.** Rs 2.4 lakh capped across ten units is not revenue. It is a state mandate to walk ten shop floors.

**MeitY is building the matchmaking layer.** A survey of 350 factories is underway, 200 of them textile and Surat-relevant, alongside a planned MSME AI Exchange to match MSMEs with AI vendors. Announced, not live.

## 7. A thesis we killed

This section exists because the discipline is the point.

**The thesis.** India's revised Schedule M pharmaceutical GMP rules took effect for MSME units on 31 December 2025. CDSCO directed state inspections from January 2026. Roughly 8,500 MSME pharma units exist, about 6,500 need upgrading, and only 1,400 to 1,700 even filed an upgradation plan. The penalty is licence suspension. Gujarat is India's densest pharma cluster. It looked like the strongest wedge available.

**What verification found.** The enforcement half held up. No 2026 extension notification exists in CDSCO's own gazette register. Gujarat FDCA cancelled 11 manufacturing licences and suspended 8 in August 2026 alone. Tamil Nadu found 4 of 46 inspected units fully compliant. Himachal, 11%.

**And then the premise failed.** Schedule M paragraph 17.2.8, read directly from the notified gazette text:

> "Data (and records for storage) **may** be recorded by electronic data-processing systems **or** by photographic **or** other reliable means."

**Paper batch records are legal and pass inspection.** The electronic-records requirements, audit trails and access control and validation, are conditional. They apply only *if* a unit chooses to go electronic, which means going digital *adds* regulatory obligations rather than discharging them. Selling eBMR as mandatory would be false, and in a cluster as tightly networked as Ahmedabad a false claim collapses once for everybody.

**A second failure, independent of the first.** Published benchmarks put single-product-line electronic batch record implementation at **9 to 12 months**, with validation, not code, as the binding constraint: URS, functional and design specification, traceability matrix, executed IQ/OQ/PQ with signed evidence, supplier audit. A six-week estimate was wrong by roughly 8x.

**What survived.** Continuous environmental monitoring, temperature, humidity, differential pressure and particle counts with tamper-evident audit-trailed logging, is genuinely required for sterile and high-risk areas, genuinely needs hardware and software, sits at GAMP category 3/4 with a fraction of eBMR's validation burden, and replaces a manual logbook inspectors already distrust.

**The lesson we carried forward:** verify the regulation against the gazette, not against vendor commentary. Most of the Schedule M content circulating online is written by people selling software.

## 8. Where a small studio wins, and where it loses

**Wins.** The plant that is too small, too unusual or too urgent for everyone above it. Three assets convert that gap: the ownership clause, which no licence vendor can copy; the fixed end date, which answers the loudest recorded complaint and is falsifiable; and cluster proximity, which is the documented strongest adoption driver in this market.

**Loses, and should decline outright.** Greenfield line automation, which is Siemens, ABB and Rockwell pulled through capex and EPC specification. Generic predictive maintenance, against a competitor with 800 plants and its own sensors. High-speed defect inspection as a product, against 1,200 units per minute at sub-150 micron. Anything procured by a listed conglomerate's CIO, where zero logos is disqualifying before price is discussed.

## 9. What we could not verify

Listed because a research document that hides its gaps is not research.

- **Gujarat Government Resolutions** on technology-acquisition percentages and caps. Referenced in policy summaries with no figures. Highest commercial value of anything unresolved here, because it sets the pricing conversation.
- **Ripik.AI total funding.** Sources contradict each other. The Rs 23.5 Cr FY25 revenue figure is the more reliable number.
- **Karnataka Industrial Policy 2025-30** technology-upgradation specifics. Secondary sources mention only stamp duty.
- **Gujarat licensed pharma unit count.** Published figures range from 649 to 3,300 depending on what is being counted. None are reliable.
- **India-specific IT spend as a percentage of revenue** for mid-market manufacturers. No credible public benchmark exists. Global mid-market manufacturing runs 3 to 5%; Indian mid-market is almost certainly below it.
- **ABB/Tata Steel and Siemens/BHEL contract values.** Single vendor-blog secondary source, not primary announcements.
- **India-specific Industry 4.0 pilot failure rate.** Does not exist in credible public form. The quotable numbers are global, dated, or vendor marketing.
