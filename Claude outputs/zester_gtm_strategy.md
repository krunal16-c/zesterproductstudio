# Zester Product Studio , GTM Strategy
**Mode: HOLD SCOPE.** The horizontal offer stands. This document makes the machine around it bulletproof.
**Date:** 5 September 2026 · **Horizon:** 90 days, then reassess
**Method:** gtm-engine (problem-based targeting, free-data-first, proof-led messaging) reviewed under plan-ceo-review discipline

---

## 0. Premise challenge

Three things had to be true for this plan to be worth writing. Two are.

**True: the gap is structural, not wishful.** Indian industrial AI is a barbell. On one end, funded product companies (Ripik ₹23.5 Cr FY25, Detect ₹46.4 Cr FY25, Infinite Uptime, SwitchOn) sell licences, which forces them to build only what repeats across a hundred plants. On the other, LTTS books deals above $10M against a $1.2bn revenue base, and Accenture now owns both Flutura and the Siemens delivery channel. Between a licence and a $1M programme there is nothing. A search for embedded or forward-deployed engineering in Indian manufacturing returns US software agencies and no domestic equivalent.

**True: the complaint you answer is documented.** PwC's March 2026 MSME study records the exact sentence: *"The consultant did a great pilot but did not have any idea about how to scale it."* Alongside it, explicit fear of vendor lock-in, hidden integration costs and subscription fees, and 91% of MSMEs saying affordability decides adoption. Roughly 70% of manufacturers have AI initiatives; about one third get past pilot. Your fixed end date and your ownership clause answer the two loudest objections in the market, and no licence vendor can copy either without breaking its own model.

**False, and you should stop saying it: that Zester has proof.** Ten products on the site are capability demonstrations, hackathon wins and internal builds. Zero are paying engagements. By gtm-engine's framing this is Phase 0, and Phase 0 says the first job is not a list, it is earning the right to sell. Section 7 is how you manufacture proof without customers.

**What happens if you do nothing:** the 80-lead campaign runs, produces a handful of conversations, and you learn the same things more slowly and with less to show for it. The campaign is not the problem. The absence of everything around it is.

## 2. Positioning

**The line:** *An engineering studio building AI that runs on factory floors, not in dashboards.*

It does three jobs at once. It says what you build, it says where it runs, and it separates you from the dashboard vendors every plant head has already been pitched. Use it on the site, the LinkedIn headline, the company page and the first line of every conversation.

**The offer, unchanged:** two engineers inside the plant for six weeks. Weeks one and two find the problem worth solving, with their people, on their data. Weeks three to six build it into production. Fixed price, fixed end date, and the client owns the code, the hardware and the documentation.

**The four clauses and the objection each one kills:**

| Clause | Objection it answers |
|---|---|
| Fixed end date | "We tried a vendor before and it never ended" |
| You own the code | Vendor lock-in, the top recorded MSME fear |
| Your team works beside us | "Nobody here will know how to run it after you go" |
| If week two shows nothing worth building, we stop | You are the only vendor in the market who will say this |

That fourth clause is the whole positioning compressed. It is also on your contact page already, which means the site and the pitch finally agree.

**What you are not.** Not a licence. Not a platform. Not a staffing firm. Not a systems integrator who wires panels. When a prospect tries to file you under one of those, the correction is the six-week shape, not a longer explanation.

## 3. Where you win and where you get destroyed

Discipline here matters more than ambition. The research is unambiguous about both halves.

**Win: the plant that is too small, too odd, or too urgent for everyone else.** A Morbi ceramics line or an Ankleshwar chemicals unit with an idiosyncratic problem is unsellable to a licence vendor, because it does not repeat. It is beneath a firm carrying 23,000 people. And the local Gujarat automation houses can wire a panel and configure a SCADA but cannot train a model or build a data pipeline. I looked: no Gujarat firm positions on embedded engineers plus edge AI plus custom build.

**Destroyed: four fights to refuse outright.**

- **Greenfield line automation.** That is Siemens, ABB and Rockwell pulled through capex and EPC specifications. You will never be specified.
- **Generic predictive maintenance.** Infinite Uptime has ~800 plants, $65M raised and its own sensors.
- **High-speed defect inspection as a product.** SwitchOn does sub-150 micron at 1,200 units/min across 170 lines.
- **Anything procured by a listed conglomerate's CIO.** Zero logos is fatal inside a procurement process.

Sell to the owner or the plant head. The moment the conversation moves to procurement, you have lost on price and prior-experience criteria, and the correct move is to shrink the deal until it sits back inside the plant head's own authority.

## 4. Targeting

The 19-account list stands. What follows sharpens which trigger you lead with, because not all of them are equal.

**Triggers that are dated and penalty-backed. Lead with these.**

**CBAM definitive phase, live since 1 January 2026.** Certificates must be bought and surrendered, first declaration 30 September 2027, penalty €100 per tonne. GTRI estimates Indian steel and aluminium exporters face 15 to 22% price pressure. The part that matters to you: a plant without verified plant-level emissions data gets assigned EU default values that run 30 to 80% above actual. That is a data collection problem with a price tag attached, and it is exactly a six-week build. From 2026 only ISO-14065 auditors can verify, which creates a partner channel as well as a wedge.

**Labour codes, in force since 21 November 2025.** Overtime now pays double, with new muster roll, wage register and overtime record formats. Every hour of unplanned overtime costs twice what it did, which doubles the return on any shift, scheduling or labour-tracking system overnight.

**Time-of-day electricity tariffs.** Maharashtra now runs a 20 to 30% discount in solar hours and a 25% surcharge at evening peak. Load-shifting has a hard, arithmetic payback. Note that Gujarat held tariffs flat for FY26, so this argument is Maharashtra-first.

**Triggers to use as context, never as the pitch.**

Capex is the one to be careful with. Private capex rose 67% to ₹7.7 lakh crore in H1 FY26, but Adani and Reliance are 28% of it and capacity utilisation sits at 75.6%. The revival is not reaching mid-market factories. Do not build a quarter's messaging on it.

**One correction you must make.** Do not tell a pharma prospect that revised Schedule M requires electronic batch records. It does not. Paragraph 17.2.8 says data *may* be recorded electronically *or* photographically *or* by other reliable means. Paper batch records are legal and pass inspection, and going electronic *adds* obligations rather than discharging them. The claim collapses the first time a buyer's consultant reads them the paragraph, and in a cluster as tightly networked as Ahmedabad it collapses once for everybody.

What is genuinely required and genuinely needs hardware plus software is **continuous environmental monitoring** for sterile and high-risk areas: temperature, humidity, differential pressure, particle counts, with tamper-evident audit-trailed logging. It replaces a manual logbook that inspectors already distrust, and it sits at GAMP category 3/4, a fraction of the validation burden of eBMR. If you sell anything into pharma, sell that.

**And do not underestimate eBMR.** Published benchmarks for a single product line run 9 to 12 months, and the binding constraint is the validation dossier, not the code. A six-week estimate there is wrong by roughly 8x.

## 5. Pricing

**Anchor against what they already recognise as the price of a system.** Indian mid-market ERP implementations run ₹20 to 60 lakh. That is the reference price in a promoter's head. A six-week sprint at **₹8 to 15 lakh** reads as sensible against it, and reads as expensive against a day rate. Never quote a day rate; it invites comparison with a local integrator at a third of the price and reframes you as bodies rather than outcomes.

**Then make the subsidy do the closing.**

| Scheme | Pays | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gujarat Technology Acquisition | 65% of cost, cap ₹50 lakh | Frame as technology acquisition, not consulting |
| Maharashtra MIISP 2025 Technology Upgradation | 50% of capital equipment, cap ₹25 lakh | Policy runs Dec 2025 to Dec 2030 |
| MSME LEAN, Intermediate / Advanced | 90% of consultant fees, capped ₹1.2L / ₹2.4L | See section 6, this is access not revenue |
| Gujarat Energy Conservation | 75% of audit cost, 25% of equipment to ₹20 lakh | Pairs with the ToD tariff pitch |

A ₹12 lakh sprint at 65% subsidy costs the plant ₹4.2 lakh. That is inside a plant head's discretionary authority and never reaches procurement, which is precisely where you want it.

**Two things the research killed.** SAMARTH Udyog gives no financial assistance to anyone, confirmed by PIB; treat it as a credibility channel only. And RPTUAS, the Schedule M upgrade subsidy, funds HVAC, water systems, cleanrooms, labs and effluent treatment. Software is not on the eligible list.

**The first three engagements.** Price at or below cost deliberately and say why: you are buying the case study, and they are buying a lower price for the risk of being first. Put it in writing. A promoter respects a straight trade and distrusts a discount with no reason attached.

## 6. Channels, ranked for 90 days

Ordered by return per rupee and per day of founder time. Roughly ₹50,000 total.

**1. Vendor Development Programmes. Near-zero cost. Do this week.**
The Vibrant Gujarat VDP puts Zydus, Lupin, GSFC, ONGC, Maruti Suzuki, Morris Garages, ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel and Elecon procurement teams in one room for an afternoon. Nothing else identified puts a studio with no logos in front of that buyer set at that price. Pair it with **DPIIT Startup India recognition**, which is free and takes a few hours: GFR Rules 170(i) and 173(i) waive the prior-experience and prior-turnover tests that would otherwise disqualify you from every PSU tender you touch.

**2. Free technical seminars at estate associations. Cost: fuel.**
Ankleshwar, Vapi, Vatva, Dahej, Panoli. Plant heads attend because it is twenty minutes away and free. Build one 90-minute talk on vision inspection or data integrity, pitched as education rather than sales, and reuse it. This is the cheapest room full of qualified buyers in India, and cluster proximity plus peer referral is documented as the strongest adoption driver for Indian MSMEs. Land three plants in one estate and the fourth is free.

**3. Founder-led LinkedIn. Cost: nothing, 3 hours a week.**
Manufacturing runs 19 to 24% P90 engagement, above Technology, and India is LinkedIn's second-largest market. Post teardowns and real measurements from your personal profile, never the company page. It compounds into channels 1 and 2 by making you a known name before you walk in.

**4. Association memberships, bought as access rather than marketing. ~₹35-45k.**
Gujarat Chemical Association (₹10,000 lifetime, ~700 members, weekly circular), CII (~₹13,000 year one at your turnover), IDMA Gujarat associate. You are buying distribution and the right to speak. Ignore the certificates.

**5. Two subcontract relationships with automation SIs. Cost: nothing.**
From the AIA India directory. Deliver one job flawlessly and cheaply. Referrals arrive in months four to six, which is outside this window, but nothing started now pays sooner.

**MSME LEAN deserves its own note, because the economics mislead.** The 90% subsidy is capped at ₹2.4 lakh across a group of four to ten enterprises over twelve months. As revenue that is not a business. As access it is exceptional: QCI or NPC empanelment puts two engineers legitimately inside four to ten factories with a government mandate and a reason to walk any shop floor. Treat LEAN as a paid customer-discovery programme and sell real sprints to the two or three units in each group with money and appetite. Empanelment needs one year of trading, ₹10 lakh average turnover and two ZED-qualified consultants. Monitor qcin.org for the next EoI round.

**Explicitly do not:** exhibit anywhere (IMTEX floor space alone starts around ₹2.34 lakh), attend iPHEX (foreign importers, wrong room), or chase NVIDIA Inception while positioned as a consulting firm, which its terms exclude.

**One timing miss to absorb.** PharmaTech Expo Gandhinagar, your home-state machinery show full of plant heads, ran 20 to 22 August 2026, two weeks before this was written. Next edition 5 to 7 August 2027. Everything in this plan is the eleven months of credibility that justify a stand there.

## 7. Manufacturing proof without customers

This is the binding constraint on every channel above, so it gets its own section.

**The retrospective backtest.** Ask for twelve months of a prospect's historical data, run your model against it, and show what it would have flagged. It costs you a week and them nothing, it converts "unknown studio" into "these people understand my plant" faster than any deck, and it is the only credential you can generate before you have clients. Make this the ask on every first call. Not a demo. Not a capabilities deck.

**Publish the teardown.** Write up what you build, with real numbers, on the site and on LinkedIn. The market's documented complaint is vendors who pilot and vanish. Published technical work is the cheapest available counter-evidence.

**Free credibility to collect this month.** DPIIT recognition (free, unlocks PSU tender eligibility), C4i4 Pune co-hosted webinar (borrows a Ministry-sanctioned centre's authority; Gujarat has no SAMARTH centre, which makes the Pune relationship more valuable, not less), MSME-DI Ahmedabad listing.

**Fix the site before any of this drives traffic.** Three nav links return 404. The published contact address is on an unregistered domain. There is no page describing the six-week engagement, so a prospect who checks you out learns you sell software products, which is not what any of these emails claim. That page is the single highest-value thing to build, and it should carry the backtest offer as its call to action.

## 8. Failure modes

The registry that matters. A failure nobody can see is the one that kills you.

| # | Failure mode | Early signal | Detected how | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Campaign reply rate under 3% | Wave 1 replies | Per-account tracking, 19 is the denominator | Trigger is wrong for segment. Re-cut by trigger before sending wave 2. |
| 2 | Replies arrive, meetings do not | High reply, low booking | Reply-to-meeting ratio | Ask is too big. Lead with the floor walk, not the sprint. |
| 3 | Meetings happen, nothing closes | 5+ meetings, 0 sprints | Pipeline stage counts | Price or proof. Test the below-cost first-three offer explicitly. |
| 4 | A sprint runs long | Week 4 with no working system | Weekly internal checkpoint | Scope was wrong in week 2. Ship the smaller thing on time. A missed end date destroys the one promise competitors cannot make. |
| 5 | Client will not give data access | Week 1 stall | Day 3 of engagement | Named in the contract as a precondition. Pause the clock, escalate to the sponsor. |
| 6 | You win work you should refuse | Greenfield or procurement-led deal | Section 3 test at qualification | Decline. Losing on price to Siemens teaches nothing and costs a quarter. |
| 7 | Both engineers on one sprint, pipeline dies | No outbound during delivery | Weekly send count | Founder does outbound during delivery. Non-negotiable. |
| 8 | Subsidy claim fails after you priced for it | Client discovers ineligibility | Verify before quoting | Never quote net-of-subsidy without written confirmation of eligibility. |
| 9 | A false compliance claim gets caught | Prospect's consultant corrects you | Silent, and that is what makes it dangerous | Do not make claims you have not read in the gazette. See section 4. |

**Number 4 and number 9 are the two that end the business.** Everything else is recoverable.

**Capacity is the hard ceiling.** Two engineers on six-week sprints is six to eight engagements a year at full utilisation. That is the real denominator for every number in this plan.

## 9. What to measure

At this volume, per-lead statistics are noise. Measure per account.

| Metric | Target | If missed |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | 5.5%+ (3.4% is average, 10.7% elite) | Below 3%, the trigger is wrong |
| Meetings booked from 80 | 4 to 8 | Below 4, the ask is too large |
| Floor walks completed | 6 in 90 days | This is the leading indicator, not meetings |
| Backtests delivered | 3 in 90 days | The proof engine is stalled |
| Paid sprints signed | 2 in 90 days | The only number that matters |
| Bounce rate | Under 2% | Verification failed |
| Spam complaints | Under 0.04% | Two or three complaints breaches this at your volume |

**The 90-day pass mark: two paid sprints and one written case study.** One reference converts the next hundred conversations. That is the entire objective.
