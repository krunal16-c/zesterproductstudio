04  /  The insight

"The consultant did a great pilot but did not have any idea about how to scale it."

PwC, Unlocking the AI Edge for MSMEs, March 2026

The same study records the other two objections: vendor lock-in and hidden subscription costs. 91% of MSMEs say affordability decides adoption.

Our engagement answers all three by construction. A fixed end date answers the first. Client ownership of the code answers the second and third. No licence business can copy the ownership clause without destroying its own model. That is a structural moat, not a feature.

05  /  The product

Six weeks, two engineers, one system in production.

Weeks 1 to 2  /  Find
Engineers on the floor with the client's people, on the client's data. Return with three candidate builds. The client picks one.
Weeks 3 to 6  /  Build
Into production, with the client's team alongside. They own the code, the hardware and the documentation.

Capability spans edge AI, computer vision, industrial IoT and applied software. The diagnosis phase exists because we do not assume we know which one a plant needs, which is exactly the assumption a product company is forced to make.

07  /  Go to market

Clusters, not campaigns.

  • Cluster density does the selling. Peer learning is the documented strongest driver of MSME technology adoption in India. Land three plants in Ankleshwar and the fourth costs nothing to acquire.
  • Vendor Development Programmes. Vibrant Gujarat puts Zydus, Lupin, GSFC, ONGC and ArcelorMittal procurement in one room for an afternoon at near-zero cost.
  • Estate association seminars. Ankleshwar, Vapi, Vatva, Dahej, Panoli. Plant heads attend because it is free and twenty minutes away.
  • Government empanelment. MSME LEAN places our engineers inside four to ten factories under a state mandate. Poor revenue, exceptional access.
  • Founder-led outbound. 80 researched leads across 19 accounts are in flight now, with per-person triggers and a five-touch sequence.
