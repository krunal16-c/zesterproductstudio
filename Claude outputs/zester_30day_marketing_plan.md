# Zester , 30 Day Growth Marketing Plan
**Founder-led. Zero budget. Zero audience. Ahmedabad.**
Built on LinkedIn's own 2026 ranking paper and three datasets totalling ~2M posts.
**Date:** 6 September 2026 · **Window:** 8 Sep to 7 Oct 2026

---

## 0. Three corrections before we start

You asked for followers, video and case studies. The research says two of those three are the wrong target, and I would rather tell you now than have you judge a working strategy by a broken metric.

**Followers are no longer how reach works.** LinkedIn moved from a relationship graph to an interest graph. Only **31%** of an average feed now comes from first-degree connections. Reach and follower count are structurally decoupled, which means a 400-follower account posting relentlessly about industrial failure can out-reach a 40,000-follower generalist. This is new in 2026 and it favours you enormously. **Chase topic authority, not followers.**

**Video is being oversold right now.** LinkedIn is publicly pushing it, and video uploads are growing double digits. But per-post video views fell **36% year on year** across every account size, because supply outran demand. Against document carousels at **7.00%** engagement, video sits at **6.00%**. LinkedIn is favouring uploads, which is not the same as favouring you. Video still has a place in this plan, and section 4 says exactly where it earns its keep.

**You cannot write case studies, because you have no clients.** What you can write is better. Edelman's study of 1,934 executives found **91%** value content that surfaces a problem they had not recognised, and **73%** say thought leadership shows the calibre of your client-facing thinking. A teardown or a failure analysis is a work sample, which is what a case study is really for. You are not missing an asset. You are using a different one.

**What 30 days actually produces:** roughly **100 to 300 followers**, 80 to 400 impressions a post, zero to three comments, and **no inbound leads**. Compound growth starts around day 35 in the best documented case study, and that founder started with 1,200 followers. First attributable inbound is a 6 to 9 month horizon. Anyone promising a thousand followers in 30 days is selling software.

## 1. Premium, defined for this market

Premium in industrial B2B is not gloss. It is restraint, specificity and real machines.

Your competitors' LinkedIn presence is beige stock photography, agency-written copy and rounded-corner infographics. Your design system, black ground, one yellow accent, radius zero, is a **visual monopoly** in that feed. Nobody else in Indian industrial AI looks like this. Hold it with total discipline and you become recognisable at thumbnail size, which is the only size that matters.

**Six rules that hold the look:**

1. **One accent, ever.** Zester Yellow and nothing else. The moment a second colour appears, the system dies.
2. **Radius zero on everything.** Cards, crops, buttons, video frames. A rounded corner is the single fastest way to look like a template.
3. **Real machines only.** No stock photography, no AI-generated factories, no illustration of a robot arm. One badly-lit photo of an actual weld beats any stock image, because your buyer can tell instantly.
4. **Yellow appears in the frame, not just the graphic.** A safety line, a machine guard, a hard hat, a lockout tag. When the accent is physically present in the photograph, the brand looks inevitable rather than applied.
5. **No music, no transitions, no zooms** on video. Machine sound is better than any track you could licence.
6. **Numbers, not adjectives.** "Cut changeover from 90 to 34 minutes" is premium. "Transforming manufacturing" is not.

## 2. The rule that should drive every design decision

**Your real distribution is a screenshot in a Gujarat industrial WhatsApp group, not the LinkedIn feed.**

Indian industrial deal flow runs through association groups, estate groups and supplier groups. A LinkedIn post is a shareable object. A 52-year-old promoter in Rajkot will not publicly comment on a post about a bearing failure. He will screenshot it and drop it in a group of eleven other promoters.

**Therefore: the first image must carry the entire insight without the caption.** If someone screenshots slide one and nothing else, the point must still land. This single constraint decides your whole visual format, and almost nobody else designs for it.

It also explains why your public engagement will look terrible while the strategy works. **Measure profile views and DMs, not likes.** Your buyers lurk. Metricool's 2026 data across 673,658 posts confirms it platform-wide: likes down 13%, comments down 17%, shares down 10%, while total engagement rose 14% and clicks rose 5%. The whole platform is shifting to silent consumption.

## 3. Content pillars

Four pillars, weighted. Every post belongs to exactly one.

| Pillar | Share | What it is | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Failure analysis** | 40% | A part that broke, why it broke, what should have been designed instead | Maximises Long Dwell, which is one of only two objectives LinkedIn's ranker optimises for. People study a broken component. Also scores highest on Edelman's "problem I had not recognised". |
| **Teardown and build** | 25% | How one of your ten systems actually works, screen by screen or sensor by sensor | The post is the work sample. 73% of buyers say this is how they judge your thinking. |
| **Cost arithmetic** | 20% | Downtime hours times rupees. Overtime at double pay. EU default values 30 to 80% above actual | Speaks to the promoter, not the engineer. Turns an engineering observation into a P&L line. |
| **Field note** | 15% | One specific thing observed on a real floor, photographed | Cheapest to produce, most human, keeps the feed from reading like a textbook |

**Deliberately excluded:** founder-journey content, motivational posts, "excited to announce", hiring posts, industry news commentary with no original observation. None of it reaches this buyer and all of it dilutes topic authority, which is now the actual ranking asset.

## 4. Formats, and where video earns its place

Engagement rate by format, from 1.3M posts across 16,645 pages:

| Format | Engagement | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Document carousel** | **7.00%** | The spine. Failure analyses, teardowns, cost breakdowns. |
| **Multi-image** | 6.45% | Before and after. Two to four frames, no text needed. |
| Native video | 6.00% | See below. Reserved, not default. |
| Single image | 5.30% | Field notes. One photograph, one paragraph. |
| Text only | 4.50% | Rare. Only when the observation needs no picture. |
| Poll | 4.20% | Highest impressions per post on pages, under 1% of supply. Use monthly. |
| Link post | 3.25% | Never. |

**Where video actually earns its place.** Not talking-head founder video, which you have no audience for and which damages perceived authority when forced. Video earns its place when **the motion is the information**: a part failing in slow motion, chips coming off a cut, a vision model drawing boxes on a live line, a pressure gauge climbing. If a still frame would say the same thing, shoot the still.

**Phone video that looks premium.** Locked-off static shots on a cheap clamp or tripod, never handheld. Four to six shots of five to eight seconds each, cut together. Macro detail: a tool edge, a weld bead, a surface finish. Natural window light or existing machine light, never the phone LED. Real machine audio captured close. Vertical 9:16 or 4:5, because your buyer is on a phone. Burn in your own subtitles, because LinkedIn's auto-captions will mangle Gujarati-accented English and every technical term you use. Total length under 60 seconds.

**What kills it:** handheld walking tours, voiceover recorded on a phone mic in a noisy plant, stock music, any on-screen text in a rounded box.

## 5. Cadence and timing

**Three posts a week: Monday, Wednesday, Friday. 8am IST.**

Three to four a week is where the data converges. Daily posting carries a **26% lower reach per post** penalty, and posting three times a week already puts you in the **top 10%** of LinkedIn users by activity, because roughly 1% of monthly actives post weekly at all.

**8am IST is India-specific and it overrides the global advice you will read everywhere.** An analysis of 220,220 Indian posts natively in IST found peak performance at 8 to 9am on weekday mornings, with Monday 9am, Monday 8am, Wednesday 8am and Friday 8am as the top four slots. Volume peaks at 10am but performance peaks at 8am, because there is less feed competition during the algorithm's initial test window. Global studies that recommend "Wednesday 4pm" are measuring US time, which lands at 1:30am in Ahmedabad.

**Post from your personal profile, not the company page.** Personal profiles get **63% higher engagement** across 673,658 posts. Only **7%** of company pages grew enough to change follower tier in a year. Keep the company page current enough not to look abandoned and spend nothing else on it.

**Two mechanical rules.** No links in the post body: text posts averaged **775 impressions against 149 for link posts** across 110,000 employee posts. Link-in-first-comment appears to be caught now too. And use zero to three hashtags at most: peer-reviewed analysis of 991 posts found two to three optimal with sharp drop-off beyond, and that **tagging a relevant person outperforms hashtags** for both reactions and comments.

## 6. The 30 days

**Week 0, the two days before you start.** Rewrite your headline to "Engineering studio building AI that runs on factory floors, not in dashboards." Rewrite the About section in first person, three paragraphs, ending with the six-week engagement. Set a banner in the brand system. Build the carousel template once so every subsequent post is a fill-in. Photograph fifteen to twenty details on any floor you can access, yours or a friend's.

|---|---|---|---|
| Mon 8 | Failure | Carousel | The weld that passed inspection and failed on site |
| Wed 10 | Cost | Carousel | What one hour of unplanned downtime actually costs |
| Fri 12 | Field note | Single image | The logbook every plant keeps and nobody reads |
| Mon 15 | Teardown | Carousel | How we predict a workplace incident seven days out |
| Wed 17 | Failure | Multi-image | Two gaskets, same spec, graded differently by two shifts |

**Daily, 30 minutes, non-negotiable.** Reply to every comment on your posts within the hour, because comments generate the dwell the ranker measures. Leave five to ten substantive comments on posts by plant heads, EHS managers and operations leaders in Gujarat and Maharashtra. Send ten to fifteen connection requests using the 200-character notes already written for your 80 leads. This manual work, not the algorithm, is where your first 300 followers come from.

## 7. First three posts, drafted

**Post 1, Monday 8 September. Failure analysis. Carousel, 7 slides.**

Slide 1, and this is the one that gets screenshotted: a photograph of a failed weld, and one line of text. *"This weld passed inspection. It failed on site four months later."*

Slides 2 to 6: where the defect was, why hot-dip galvanizing hid it, what post-galvanizing NDE can and cannot detect, what inspection before the bath would have caught, what site rework costs against shop rework.

Slide 7: *"We build inspection systems for fabrication shops. Zester Product Studio, Ahmedabad."*

Caption, first two lines only visible before the fold:

> Galvanizing covers the weld. Whatever the bath hides gets found on site, by which point rework costs several times what it would have in the shop.
>
> Most tower fabricators inspect after the bath because that is when the part looks finished. The defect is already sealed under zinc by then.
>
> The uncomfortable part is that nobody is doing anything wrong. The sequence is standard. It is just that the one moment inspection is cheap sits before the one moment the part looks inspectable.
>
> How many towers come back for rework at your shop?

**Post 2, Wednesday 10 September. Cost arithmetic. Carousel, 6 slides.**

Slide 1: black slide, yellow number, nothing else. *"Rs 1,40,000."* Second line, small: *"One hour of unplanned downtime on a mid-size line."*

Slides 2 to 5: build the number. Lost output at contribution margin. Restart and stabilisation time. Overtime to recover the schedule, now at double pay under the new labour codes. The order you did not quote because the line was full.

Slide 6: *"Most plants track the first line. Almost none track all four."*

Caption ends: *"Which of these four does your plant actually measure?"*

**Post 3, Friday 12 September. Field note. Single image.**

A photograph of a real handwritten logbook on a plant desk, shot in natural light, yellow somewhere in frame.

> Every plant I walk into has one of these.
>
> Three or four years of temperature readings, pressure checks, near-misses, shift handover notes. Written carefully, every single shift, by people who take it seriously.
>
> And then nothing reads it. It gets filled in for the inspector and filed for the auditor.
>
> There is more signal in one of these books than in most of the dashboards sold to Indian factories last year. The difference is that a dashboard tells you what happened yesterday and this book, read properly, tells you what happens next week.
>
> We built a system that reads exactly this kind of record and puts a probability on the next seven days.
>
> What does your plant do with its logbooks?

## 8. Production system

**Batch on Sunday, two to three hours.** Write all three captions. Build all three carousels from the template. Schedule for 8am Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Leave one slot loose in case something worth photographing happens midweek.

**Shoot in bulk.** Every time you are on any factory floor, take twenty photographs of detail: tool edges, weld beads, gauges, control panels, logbooks, safety lines, wear patterns. You will never regret having a library and you will always regret needing an image on a Tuesday night.

**Carousel spec.** 1080x1350 portrait, exported as PDF for LinkedIn native document upload. Seven to nine slides. Slide one carries the whole insight. Final slide is a plain sign-off, never a hard sell.

**One reusable asset worth building in week one:** a failure-analysis template with fixed slots for the photograph, the observation, the mechanism, the cost and the fix. After that, each post is forty minutes rather than three hours.

## 9. What to measure

Ignore likes. They are falling platform-wide and your buyers do not use them.

| Metric | 30-day target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Profile views** | 300+ | The lurking buyer's only visible trace |
| **DMs and connection acceptances** | 40%+ acceptance | Your actual pipeline |
| **Posts published** | 13 of 13 | Consistency is the only thing fully in your control |
| **Comments received** | 15 across all posts | Drives dwell, which the ranker measures directly |
| **Impressions per post** | 80 to 400 | Expect a dip in week two. Normal. |
| Followers | 100 to 300 | Reported, not optimised for |
| Inbound leads | 0 | Correct at 30 days. Not a failure. |

**Week 5 decision point.** Compare the three pillars by impressions and profile views, not likes. Kill the weakest, double the strongest. That is the entire point of running 13 posts across four pillars in month one: to buy the data that tells you what month two should be.

## 10. What will not work

Stated plainly so you do not spend a week discovering each one.

- **The company page.** 7% of pages grew a tier in a year. Keep it tidy and forget it.
- **Links in posts.** 775 impressions against 149. You have nothing worth linking to yet anyway.
- **Hashtags as a growth lever.** Two or three maximum, or none. Tag a person instead.
- **Daily posting.** 26% lower reach per post, four times the work.
- **Talking-head founder video** with no audience and no reason to watch.
- **Motivational or founder-journey content.** Wrong buyer entirely.
- **Expecting inbound in 30 days.** The ranker holds a memory of your last 1,000 impressions. Until you have generated consistent topical impressions, LinkedIn does not know what you are about.
- **Judging this by public engagement.** Your buyers lurk. The most common way this strategy fails is that it is abandoned at week three while working.

## 11. Where this connects to revenue

This plan does not generate leads in 30 days and it is not supposed to. It does three things that make everything else in the GTM plan work.

It makes you **known before you walk in**. A plant head who has seen three of your failure analyses is a fundamentally different conversation from a cold visit. Edelman found **53%** of executives say strong thought leadership diminishes the importance of brand recognition, which is the single most useful finding available to a two-person firm with no logos.

It makes the **estate association seminars** possible. You cannot offer to speak at Ankleshwar or Vatva with an empty profile. Thirteen published teardowns is the credential that gets you the room.

It gives the **80-lead outbound campaign** a warm surface. Someone who receives your email, looks you up, and finds a feed of specific industrial failure analysis converts at a different rate from someone who finds an empty profile and a 404.

**The honest sequence is: content makes outbound work, outbound produces the first sprint, the first sprint produces the case study, and the case study is what finally makes content produce inbound.** Month one is the first link in that chain, not a shortcut past it.
